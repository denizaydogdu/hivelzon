# The Parrot Who Told Everything
> A2-K03 · Mizah · H-069 pilot metni (taslak, kullanıcı onayı bekliyor).
> Kurallar: cümle ≤ 12 kelime; past simple, going to, must / mustn't, sıklık zarfları, karşılaştırma. İşaretler: docs/KITAPLIK.md §6.2.

## [c1] The Rules

### [p01]
Max had one rule: never say anything near the parrot.

But that Saturday, he forgot it.

Max was sixteen, and he wanted a guitar. Not just any guitar. He wanted the red one in the music shop on the corner. He looked at it every day after school. It cost two hundred. Max had one hundred and forty.

So when Mrs Bell from next door called him, he listened carefully.

"Can you look after the twins on Saturday?" she asked. "We're going to a wedding. We're going to be back at ten. I can pay you sixty."

Sixty! Max did the maths very quickly.

"Of course," he said. "It's going to be easy."

### [p02]
At nine o'clock on Saturday, Max knocked on the Bells' door. Mr Bell opened it. He was a tall man with a tired face.

Behind him, two small faces watched Max. Finn and Nora were seven. They had the same red hair and the same smile. It was a dangerous smile.

"Hi," said Max. "I'm going to look after you today."

Nora looked at Finn. Finn looked at Nora. Then they ran up the stairs and disappeared.

Max followed Mr Bell into the living room. On the wall, there was an old brown guitar. It looked lonely.

"Do you play?" asked Max.

Mr Bell looked at it. "Not anymore," he said quietly.

### [p03]
Mrs Bell gave Max a long list. "These are the house rules," she said.

The children must have lunch at twelve.

They mustn't eat sweets before lunch.

They must have a bath at six.

They must be in bed at eight.

Biscuit, the cat, mustn't go outside.

"And one more thing," said Mr Bell. He pointed at a big cage near the window. A green bird sat inside. He looked at Max with one orange eye.

"This is Captain. He repeats everything. And I mean everything. So be careful with your words. At night, he always sleeps in the children's room."

"Hello, Captain," said Max.

"Hello, Captain!" said the bird. He sounded exactly like Max.

### [p04]
The Bells left at half past nine. The house was very quiet. It was too quiet.

"Finn? Nora?" Max called.

Nobody answered. He looked in the kitchen. He looked in the bathroom. He looked under the beds and inside the cupboards. The children weren't anywhere.

Ten minutes later, he still couldn't find them. His heart went faster and faster.

Max sat down on the sofa. He put his head in his hands.

"This is a disaster!" he said.

"This is a disaster!" said Captain happily.

Then the sofa laughed.

### [p05]
Two red heads came up from behind the sofa.

"Found you!" said Max. He was angry, but he was happy, too.

"Found you!" said Captain.

The children thought this was very funny. They laughed, and Captain laughed with them. His laugh sounded exactly like Mr Bell's laugh.

Max turned to the bird. "Please be quiet," he said.

"Please be quiet," said Captain.

Max closed his eyes. Only twelve more hours, he thought. Sixty. A red guitar.

When he opened his eyes, the children weren't there again.

Then he heard a strange noise from the kitchen. A big white cloud came out of the door.

## [c2] Pancakes and Bubbles

### [p06]
The kitchen was white. The floor was white. The table was white. Even Biscuit was white, and Biscuit was usually a brown cat.

In the middle of the kitchen stood two little white ghosts. They held an empty bag of flour.

"We're making pancakes," said Finn.

"For you!" said Nora.

Max opened his mouth. He wanted to shout. But then he looked at their faces. They looked very pleased with themselves.

He sat down for a moment. "OK," he said. "We're going to cook. Together. And slowly."

### [p07]
Cooking with two seven-year-olds was harder than any exam.

Finn dropped the eggs on the floor. Nora put salt in the milk. The first one flew up and stayed on the ceiling.

"Oh no, not again!" said Max.

"Oh no, not again!" said Captain from the living room. He sounded happier than Max.

But the next one was perfect. It was round and warm and golden. The children ate it with honey. They said it was the best pancake in the world.

At twelve, they had lunch. At one, Max cleaned the kitchen. At three, he was very tired.

At four, the house was quiet again. Too quiet.

### [p08]
Max found the children in the bathroom.

The bath was full of bubbles. They were on the floor and on the walls. They were even on the mirror. And in the middle of the bath sat Biscuit. He did not look happy.

"Biscuit needs a bath," said Nora. "He's white now."

"Cats hate water!" said Max.

Biscuit agreed. He jumped out of the bath and ran down the stairs. The back door was open.

"Biscuit! Come back, you silly cat!" shouted Max.

"Silly cat!" shouted Captain. He was even happier now.

### [p09]
Biscuit was faster than Max. He ran across the garden and under a bush.

Max lay on the wet grass and held out his hand. "Come on, Biscuit," he said. "Please. I'm going to give you some fish."

After ten minutes, Biscuit came out. Max carried him back into the house. His socks were wet. His shirt was wet. His hair was still full of flour.

He read the list of rules again. Biscuit mustn't go outside. Max sat down on the stairs.

"I'm never going to get that guitar," he said.

"Never going to get that guitar!" said Captain.

### [p10]
At eight o'clock, Finn and Nora were in bed. But they didn't sleep. Outside, the wind was strong.

"I don't like the wind," said Nora in a small voice.

Max carried Captain into their room. The bird was quiet. For once, he didn't say anything. But his eyes were open.

Max sat on the floor between the two beds.

"Listen," he said softly. "You two are very brave. You cooked for me. You gave a cat a bath. That's brave."

Nora smiled. Then Max sang an old song. His grandmother often sang it to him.

"Little boat, little boat, the moon is your light. Little boat, little boat, come home tonight."

Slowly, the children closed their eyes.

Then Max heard a car outside. He looked at the clock. It was only nine. The Bells were home early!

## [c3] Captain Speaks

### [p11]
Max ran downstairs. He looked around the living room. There was flour on the sofa. There was a pancake on the ceiling. There was a white cat on the chair.

The front door opened.

"Hello, Max!" said Mrs Bell. "How was your day? Were the children good?"

"Yes!" said Max. "They were great. Everything was fine."

Upstairs, somebody spoke. It was Captain. The bedroom door was open.

"This is a disaster!" said Captain.

Mrs Bell looked at Max. Max looked at the floor.

### [p12]
"Oh no, not again!" said Captain.

Mr Bell looked up. He saw the pancake above his head.

"Silly cat!" said Captain.

Mrs Bell looked at Biscuit. Biscuit was still a little white.

Max's face was redder than a tomato. He wanted to disappear.

"I'm sorry," he said. "I can explain. There was flour. Then there were bubbles. Then the cat went into the garden."

"Never going to get that guitar!" said Captain.

"You don't have to pay me," said Max quietly.

### [p13]
But Captain was not finished. He walked up and down in his cage. Then he spoke again. His voice was softer now.

"You are very brave."

Mrs Bell stopped. "Who said that?"

Then Captain began to sing. He sang very badly. But everyone knew the words.

"Little boat, little boat, the moon is your light…"

Mr Bell's face changed. He looked at Max.

"Where did he learn that song?" he asked.

"From me," said Max. "I sang it to Finn and Nora. My grandmother taught it to me."

Two small faces appeared at the top of the stairs.

"Max made pancakes!" said Finn. "Max is the best!" said Nora.

### [p14]
Mr Bell went into the living room. He came back with the old guitar.

"My mother sang that song, too," he said. "I often played it on this guitar. But my hands are too slow now. This guitar needs a new player."

He gave it to Max. Max couldn't speak.

Mrs Bell laughed and gave him sixty. "Are you free next Saturday?" she asked. "And every Saturday?"

"Every Saturday!" said Captain.

That night, Max played the guitar for the first time. He played badly, but he played for hours.

And he had a new rule. Always say nice things near the parrot.
