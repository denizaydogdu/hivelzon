# The Lemon Cake Mystery
> A2-K01 · Gizem · H-071 metni (taslak, kullanıcı onayı bekliyor).
> Kurallar: cümle ≤ 12 kelime; past simple, karşılaştırma, mutfak fiilleri, going to; make / do. İşaretler: docs/KITAPLIK.md §6.2.

## [c1] The Empty Box

### [p01]
The day before the contest, the famous recipe disappeared.

Well, two days before. But it felt like one.

Elif was fifteen. Every summer, she worked in her family's small restaurant. It was in Ayvalık, on a street near the sea. In the mornings, Elif cut bread and carried plates. In the evenings, she did the dishes.

The most famous thing in the restaurant was a cake. It was Aunt Füsun's lemon cake. People came from other towns for it. They always ate one piece and asked for a second.

Nobody knew the recipe. Nobody except one old card.

And now the card was missing. Who took it? And why?

### [p02]
On Sunday, there was a summer cake contest in the town square. Every restaurant brought its best cake. This year, Elif wanted to enter with the lemon cake.

"I'm too old for contests," said Aunt Füsun. She laughed. "You go, Elif. You can make it."

Aunt Füsun never wrote anything down. She cooked with her hands, not with paper. But there was one card in an old wooden box. It was the recipe for the lemon cake. Everyone in the kitchen knew about that card.

On Friday morning, Elif opened the box. She wanted to read the card again.

The box was empty.

### [p03]
Elif looked under the box. She looked behind the oven and in every drawer. Nothing.

Then she looked at the kitchen table. There was white flour on it. And in the flour, there were marks from a hand. Somebody touched the box with white hands.

"Everyone here has white hands," Elif thought.

She went to her room and found an old notebook. On the first page, she wrote three words: "Find the recipe."

Under them, she wrote: "Clue one: flour on the table."

A real detective needed clues. And a real detective needed suspects.

### [p04]
That evening, Elif gave Limon his dinner. Limon was the restaurant's cat. He was fat and yellow, like a lemon. In the day, he slept in a basket behind the kitchen door. At night, he always slept on Aunt Füsun's bed.

Elif looked into his basket. There was something white under the cat.

"Move, Limon," she said. Limon didn't move. Cats never move when you ask.

Elif picked him up slowly. Under him, there was a piece of card. It was half of a card, with old, careful writing.

She read the first line. It said: "Lemon Cake."

## [c2] Three Suspects

### [p05]
Elif read the half card again and again. It said "four eggs," "one glass of sugar" and "two lemons." The other half was missing.

Somebody broke the card in two. Did they keep the other half?

She opened her notebook. "Clue two: half a card in Limon's basket."

Then she wrote three names under the clues. Bora. Theo. Mr Selim.

She liked all three people. That was the worst part.

### [p06]
Bora was Elif's cousin. He was seventeen, and he loved his phone. He took photos of every plate in the restaurant. Then he put them online.

"Did you take the recipe card?" Elif asked him.

"Why? I don't need a card," said Bora. "I only need photos."

He showed her a photo of the lemon cake. It had fifty thousand likes.

But Elif noticed something. Bora's hands were white with flour.

"I helped with the bread this morning," he said quickly.

Too quickly? Elif wrote it down.

### [p07]
Theo was the new waiter. He came from England, and he was learning Turkish. He was polite and quiet. He always carried a small blue notebook. All day, he wrote new words in it.

"Theo, were you in the kitchen yesterday?" Elif asked.

"Yes. Every morning," said Theo. His Turkish was slow but careful. "I come very early."

"Why so early? The restaurant opens at ten."

Theo smiled, but he didn't answer. He closed his notebook a little faster.

### [p08]
Mr Selim had the cake shop across the street. He was the kindest man in Ayvalık. He gave sweets to every child.

"Your aunt's recipe? I'd love to see it!" he laughed. "But I make my own cakes. And mine are sweeter."

Elif compared her suspects in her notebook. Bora was the busiest. Theo was the quietest. Mr Selim was the kindest. Nobody looked like a thief.

Later that evening, Theo left his notebook on a table. It was open. Elif didn't want to read it. But she saw one page.

There was a drawing of a lemon. Next to it, there was a time: six in the morning.

## [c3] Three Bad Cakes

### [p09]
Elif had no card. But she had half a card and a good memory. She watched Aunt Füsun make the cake many times.

"I can do this," she said. Then she laughed. "No. I can make this."

On Saturday morning, she made her first cake. She used two lemons, like the card said. After forty minutes, she took it out of the oven.

It looked perfect. She ate a piece.

It was very sour! Her face looked like Limon's face in the rain.

"It's more sour than a lemon," said Bora. He took a photo.

### [p10]
For the second cake, Elif used one lemon and less butter. It wasn't sour this time. But it was dry. It was drier than an old shoe.

For the third cake, she added more sugar. It was sweet. It was much too sweet. It was sweeter than Mr Selim's cakes.

Three cakes, and three bad cakes. Elif sat down on the kitchen floor. The contest was tomorrow.

### [p11]
Aunt Füsun came into the kitchen. She tried a little piece of each cake. Then she started to make lunch.

Elif watched her. Aunt Füsun put oil in a pan, then onions, then tomatoes. She added a little of this and a little of that. She never looked at a card, a book or a phone.

Then Elif understood something. She opened her notebook. "Clue three: Aunt Füsun never cooks from paper."

So why was there a card in the box?

### [p12]
In the early evening, Elif heard voices behind the restaurant. Theo and Aunt Füsun sat at a small table outside.

"Good evening, Theo," said Aunt Füsun slowly. She said it in English!

Theo answered her in Turkish. Then they both laughed.

So that was Theo's secret! Every morning at six, they had a lesson. Aunt Füsun taught Theo Turkish. Theo taught Aunt Füsun English. The lemon was for her lessons. She paid him with cake.

Elif put a line through Theo's name.

That night, Elif couldn't sleep. At midnight, she went down for some water. There was a light in the kitchen.

Aunt Füsun stood by the oven. She didn't look surprised.

"Are you ready?" she asked. In English.

## [c4] The Real Recipe

### [p13]
"Ready for what?" asked Elif.

"For the real recipe," said Aunt Füsun.

She took something out of her pocket. It was the other half of the card.

Elif's mouth opened. "You took it?"

"I hid it," said Aunt Füsun. "On Thursday night. Limon helped me."

Elif remembered the flour on the table. Aunt Füsun's hands always had flour on them. And Limon slept in Aunt Füsun's room every night.

"He loves paper," said Aunt Füsun. "He took one half to his basket. I'm sorry about that."

### [p14]
"But why?" asked Elif.

Aunt Füsun put the two pieces together on the table. "Read it," she said.

Elif read it. Then she read it again. Three lemons. Two glasses of salt. No butter at all.

"This is wrong!" she said. "This makes a terrible cake!"

"Of course it does," said Aunt Füsun. "Every year, somebody wants my recipe. Every year, somebody opens that box. So I wrote a wrong card. It's a trap for people with long noses."

"So where is the real recipe?"

Aunt Füsun showed her two hands. Then she touched her head. "Here. I didn't want you to learn from paper. I wanted you to learn with your eyes. Today you watched me. You saw everything."

### [p15]
They baked all night. Aunt Füsun didn't use a glass or a spoon. She showed Elif everything.

"Feel the butter. It must be soft. Smell the lemon. Look at the colour."

Elif watched her hands and did the same. The first cake was good. The second cake was better.

At three o'clock, Elif had an idea. She went into the garden. She came back with a few green leaves of mint. She put a little mint in the third cake.

Aunt Füsun tried it. She closed her eyes for a long time.

"That's not my cake," she said at last. "That's your cake."

### [p16]
On Sunday, the town square was full of people and cakes. The judges tried every cake slowly.

Mr Selim won with a big chocolate cake. Elif and Aunt Füsun came second.

For a moment, Elif was sad. Then she looked at Aunt Füsun. Aunt Füsun was laughing, so Elif laughed, too.

"Second is good," said Aunt Füsun. "Next year, you're going to win. And I'm going to write a new wrong card."

Bora took a photo of them. That evening, it got more likes than the cake.

That night, Elif opened her notebook for the last time. Under "Find the recipe," she wrote one more line.

"Found. It was in Aunt Füsun's hands. Now it's in mine, too."
