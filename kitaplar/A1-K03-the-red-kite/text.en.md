# The Red Kite
> A1-K03 · Macera · H-071 metni (taslak, kullanıcı onayı bekliyor).
> Kurallar: cümle ≤ 8 kelime, present simple, yan cümle yok. İşaretler: docs/KITAPLIK.md §6.2.

## [c1] The Wind

### [p01]
Today, Ravi's red kite flies over the town.

Ravi is nine. He lives in a town by the sea. His home is on a hill. It has a small balcony with flowers.

Ravi and his dad make the kite. It is red and yellow.

Mum is on the balcony. "Lunch is at twelve, Ravi!"

"OK, Mum!"

It is Sunday morning. The wind is very strong. Ravi runs to the park.

Where does the kite go today?

### [p02]
The park is on top of the hill. There are tall trees and green grass.

Ravi holds his kite. It goes up, up, up. It dances in the blue sky.

"Look at my kite!" says Ravi.

Then the wind is very strong. Ravi's hand opens. The kite flies away!

"Stop!" says Ravi. But a kite can't hear.

It flies over the trees. It goes down the hill. Ravi runs after it.

### [p03]
Ravi runs into the market. There are apples, oranges and bananas.

Mr Das sells fruit here. He has a big white hat. He knows Ravi.

"Mr Das! My kite! Can you see it?"

Mr Das looks up. "It goes to the river."

"Oh no! The river is very far."

Mr Das smiles. He gives Ravi an orange. "Don't be sad. The wind turns at twelve."

Ravi does not know this. But he says thank you. Then he runs on.

### [p04]
Now Ravi is on a long street. Cars and bikes go up and down.

An old yellow tram comes down the street. It is very slow.

Ravi looks up. Where is his kite?

Then he sees something red. It is on top of the tram!

It goes down the street. It does not stop.

## [c2] The River

### [p05]
"Wait! Wait!" calls Ravi. He runs and runs.

The tram stops at the corner. Ravi gets on. He sits near a window.

The tram goes down, down, down. Ravi can see pink houses and blue houses. He can't see his kite. It is over his head.

Soon it stops again. Ravi gets off. He looks up.

The kite is not there! The wind takes it again.

### [p06]
The kite flies over a high wall. Ravi can't go over the wall.

There are steps near it. Ravi runs down the steps. There are many, many steps!

On the wall, there is a black cat. It has big yellow eyes. It watches the kite, too.

"Do you see my kite?" asks Ravi.

The cat looks at the sky. Then it looks at the river.

### [p07]
Now Ravi is at the river. There are big boats and small boats.

There is no bridge here. Ravi can't go over the river.

Then he sees the kite. It is on a white boat!

The boat is on the other side.

### [p08]
"Hello!" calls Ravi. "That is my kite!"

But no one hears him. The wind is very strong.

Ravi sits down. He is tired. His legs hurt.

He looks at his watch. It is eleven o'clock. Lunch is at twelve.

Then the white boat moves. It goes down the river.

And the red kite goes with it!

## [c3] Home

### [p09]
Ravi stands up. There is a small green ferry. It goes to the other side.

"Can I come, please?" asks Ravi.

A kind woman smiles. "Yes, you can."

The ferry goes over the river. The wind is in Ravi's hair. He can see the white boat. The kite is still on it.

### [p10]
Soon they are on the other side. Ravi jumps off. He runs near the river.

The white boat stops, too. Ravi can see the red kite.

"Please! My kite!" calls Ravi.

Then the town clock rings. One, two, three… twelve.

And the wind turns.

The kite goes up into the sky. Now it flies back over the river!

"No!" says Ravi. "Not again!"

### [p11]
Ravi runs after the kite again. Here there is a long bridge. He runs over it.

The kite flies over the market. Mr Das sees Ravi. He laughs. "The wind turns at twelve!"

The kite flies up the hill. Ravi runs up the hill, too. His legs are very tired.

He knows this street. He knows this tree. He knows this house.

It is his street!

### [p12]
The red kite is in some flowers. They are on a small balcony.

It is Ravi's balcony!

Mum is there. She holds the red kite. She laughs.

"Ravi! Your kite is home first!"

Ravi laughs, too. He is tired, hot and very happy. He thinks of Mr Das.

The morning wind goes to the river. At twelve, it turns. It brings the kite home.

Mum looks at the clock. It is twelve o'clock.

"Lunch is ready," she says.
