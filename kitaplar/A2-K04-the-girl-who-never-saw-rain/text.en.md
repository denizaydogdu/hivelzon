# The Girl Who Never Saw Rain
> A2-K04 · Bilimkurgu/fantastik · H-071 metni (taslak, kullanıcı onayı bekliyor).
> Kurallar: cümle ≤ 12 kelime; past simple, karşılaştırma (heavier, bigger than, the same as), going to, hava ve duyu sözcükleri. İşaretler: docs/KITAPLIK.md §6.2.

## [c1] Moon Girl

### [p01]
Nia was born on the Moon, and she wanted to see rain.

It was the year 2091. Nia was thirteen. She lived in Lunaria, a small town under a glass roof. Her mum and dad were scientists there.

On the Moon, the sky was always black. The weather was always the same. Every day was clean, quiet and dry.

From her bedroom window, Nia could see the Earth. It was blue and white, and very far away.

"What is rain really like?" she often asked.

Her parents remembered rain, but they never agreed. Mum said it was cold. Dad said it was warm. Nia wanted to know for herself.

### [p02]
Nia had a friend on Earth. His name was Leo, and he was thirteen too. They wrote to each other every week.

Leo and his grandma had a farm in a hot, dry valley.

His letters were full of things Nia never saw. Dogs. Wind. Trees taller than houses.

Her favourite letter was about rain.

"Rain is not only water," Leo wrote. "It has a smell. Grandma says the earth smells sweet after rain. You can't smell it anywhere else."

Nia read that letter ten times. A smell!

"I'm going to smell it one day," she wrote back. "I promise."

### [p03]
But going to Earth was not easy. The Earth was much bigger than the Moon. Everything there was six times heavier.

"Your legs are going to feel like stone," said Dr Ansel.

So every morning, Nia trained in the sports room. She walked in heavy shoes. She ran on a machine that made her body heavier.

It was hard. Some days she wanted to stop.

Then she remembered Leo's letter. The sweet smell of the earth after rain.

And she put on the heavy shoes again.

### [p04]
One evening, Mum came home with a big smile.

"Nia, sit down," she said. "I have news."

Mum put a small blue card on the table.

"Is that…?" asked Nia.

"Yes. It's your ticket to Earth. We're going to visit Leo's family for two weeks."

Nia jumped up so high that her head touched the roof. On the Moon, that was easy.

She wrote to Leo that night. "I'm coming! Is it going to rain?"

Leo's answer came three days later. It was very short.

"Come," he wrote. "But there is no rain here. The last rain was forty days ago."

## [c2] Too Much Sky

### [p05]
The spaceship landed on a hot afternoon.

Nia stood up, and her legs felt like stone. Dr Ansel was right. Her small bag felt heavier than a big suitcase.

Then the door opened, and she saw the sky.

It was not black. It was blue, and it was everywhere. It had no roof and no end.

Nia stopped at the door. Her heart was fast. She held Dad's hand hard.

"It's too big," she said quietly. "Where does it stop?"

"It doesn't," said Dad. "That's the best part."

Nia didn't think so. She looked at the ground all the way to the car.

### [p06]
Leo was taller than in his photos, and he laughed a lot.

His grandma, Edda, gave Nia a long, warm hug. She smelled of bread.

The farm was beautiful, but it was very dry. The fields were yellow and brown. The ground had long lines in it, like an old face.

"Forty days," said Grandma Edda. She looked up at the empty sky. "Forty days and not one drop."

Nia looked up too, but only for a second. The sky was still too big for her.

### [p07]
The next morning, Leo showed Nia the farm.

There were long black pipes on the ground. They went between the plants like snakes. Every few metres, there was a small metal head on top. One of them went right past the house, under Nia's window.

"What are those?" asked Nia.

"Our water pipes," said Leo happily. "I built them with Grandma. At night, they throw water over the fields."

Leo turned a red wheel near the house. Water jumped out of the little heads. It flew up and fell on the tomatoes.

Nia put her hand in it. The water was warm, like bath water.

"It's like rain!" she said.

"Almost," said Leo. "But real rain is better." He turned the wheel off. "We have to save water. We only use them at night."

### [p08]
That evening, they watched the weather report on Grandma's old computer. There was a map with a big grey cloud on it.

"Rain is coming on Friday," said the weather report.

Leo shouted. Grandma Edda laughed.

"Friday is in three days," said Leo. "Perfect, Moon Girl!"

That night, Nia sat at her window. Outside, the sky was full of stars. She looked up for one minute. Then for two minutes.

"Three more days," she said to the stars.

## [c3] Friday

### [p09]
On Wednesday, Nia looked at the sky for ten minutes. On Thursday, she looked for half an hour. It was getting easier.

Leo taught her about clouds.

"The small white ones are nice," he said. "But they never bring rain. You need big grey ones. Dark and heavy, like old blankets."

On Friday morning, the sky was white and flat. Nia ran outside early.

Then the clouds came. They got darker and bigger. Soon they were the biggest clouds Nia ever saw.

The wind was cooler now. The trees moved.

"Here it comes," said Grandma Edda.

### [p10]
Everyone waited.

Grandma put buckets in the garden. Leo and Nia stood in the dry field. Nia turned her face up to the sky.

They waited for ten minutes. Then an hour. Then all afternoon.

Nothing fell. Not one drop.

In the evening, the wind changed. Slowly, the grey clouds moved away to the east. Behind them, the sky was clear and hot again.

"It happens," said Grandma Edda softly. "The clouds were only passing."

Nia went inside without a word.

### [p11]
In her room, Nia sat on the bed.

She opened her notebook and wrote. Only five days are left. Then we are going to fly home.

A tear fell on the page. Then another one.

Someone knocked on the door. It was Leo.

"Are you OK?" he asked.

"I'm fine," said Nia. But her voice was not fine.

Leo looked at her for a long time. Then he closed the door quietly.

A minute later, Nia heard the back door. Then she heard nothing.

### [p12]
Late at night, Nia woke up.

There was a sound on the roof. Tap. Tap, tap.

She sat up in bed. The sound got faster. Tap-tap-tap-tap. It was soft, and it was everywhere.

Nia's heart jumped. She knew this sound from old films.

She ran to the window. It was dark outside, but the glass was wet.

Nia ran down the stairs in her pyjamas. She opened the back door.

Everything was wet.

## [c4] The Smell of Rain

### [p13]
Water fell on Nia's hair, her face and her arms.

She laughed and turned around and around.

"Rain!" she shouted. "It's raining!"

It was warmer than she expected. It was softer, too.

Then Leo walked out of the dark. He was wet, and his shoes were very dirty.

"Is this it?" asked Nia. "Is this real rain?"

"Yes," said Leo. But he didn't look at her.

### [p14]
Nia closed her eyes. She breathed in slowly and deeply.

She waited for the smell. The sweet smell of the earth from his letter.

But there was nothing. The air smelled of water and a little of metal. It smelled the same as the water in Lunaria.

Nia opened her eyes and looked up.

Above the house, she saw stars. Lots of stars, and only one small cloud. And the water did not come from above. It came from the side, from somewhere near her window.

Then the water stopped.

Everything was quiet. Leo didn't move.

They sat on the wet steps for almost an hour.

### [p15]
Far away, there was a low sound, like a big drum.

The wind changed. It was colder now, much colder. The small cloud was not alone anymore. A dark wall of clouds came over the hill.

The first drop hit Nia on the nose. It was cold! Then another, and another. Then the whole sky opened.

This water was different. It was heavier and colder, and it came from high above.

Then the smell came. It came up from the dry ground, sweet and deep. It smelled like bread, old wood and something new.

Nobody needed to tell Nia. She knew.

"This is rain," she said softly. "This is the real rain."

### [p16]
Leo stood next to her. He turned his face up to the sky too.

"I'm sorry," he said. "The first rain was me. I turned on the pipes. The one under your window."

"I know," said Nia. "Your rain was warmer. And it had no smell."

"You were so sad. I only wanted…"

"I know," she said again, and she smiled. "It was the kindest rain in the world."

They laughed and stayed outside until they were very, very wet. Grandma Edda came out with an umbrella. Then she closed it and stood with them.

That night, Nia wrote in her notebook. Rain is colder than you think. It is heavier, too. And it smells better than anything on the Moon.
