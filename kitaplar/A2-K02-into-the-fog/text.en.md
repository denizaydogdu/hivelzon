# Into the Fog
> A2-K02 · Macera · H-071 metni (taslak, kullanıcı onayı bekliyor).
> Kurallar: cümle ≤ 12 kelime; past simple, must / have to / should, yön tarifleri, hava sözcükleri, because / so. İşaretler: docs/KITAPLIK.md §6.2.

## [c1] The Flower

### [p01]
When the fog came down, the path disappeared.

But that happened later. In the morning, the sky was blue and clear.

Paloma Ruiz was fourteen. She was on a school trip in the mountains with her class. They stayed in an old wooden house above the trees.

Paloma loved her camera. It was an old camera from her grandfather. She took photos of everything: rocks, birds, clouds and her friends' shoes.

"Paloma, put that camera down," said her friend Jonas. "Mrs Berg is talking."

Jonas was tall and quiet. He always listened to teachers. Paloma usually didn't.

### [p02]
Mrs Berg was small, with a big voice and a red hat. She knew the mountain well.

"Today we're going to walk to the lake," she said. "Listen carefully, because there are four rules."

"One. Stay on the path. Always."

"Two. Never walk alone. Stay with a friend."

"Three. The weather can change very quickly here. If the fog comes, stop and stay together."

At that moment, Paloma saw a butterfly on a rock. It was bright blue. Slowly, she held up her camera.

"Four," said Mrs Berg. "If you are lost, listen. Follow the b…"

Click. The butterfly flew away. Paloma looked at her photo happily.

"…and you're going to be home before dark," finished Mrs Berg.

Paloma did not hear rule four. But Jonas did.

### [p03]
They walked for two hours, up through the trees. At the top, there was a lake. It was as blue as the sky.

They ate lunch next to the water. Then Mrs Berg looked at the sky. "We should go back now," she said. "Some clouds are coming."

On the way down, Paloma walked at the back with Jonas. Then she saw it. Between two rocks, there was a small white flower like a star.

"Jonas, look! I have to take a photo of that."

"We have to stay on the path," said Jonas.

"It's only ten steps. Come on. Two minutes."

Jonas looked at the class. They were not far away. "OK," he said. "Two minutes."

### [p04]
They left the path and climbed down to the rocks. Paloma took photo after photo.

"One more," she said. "The light is better now."

But the light was not better. It was whiter. Then it was grey.

Jonas looked up. "Paloma," he said quietly. "Where is the path?"

Paloma stood up. Everything was white. She could not see the class or the path. She could only see Jonas and the flower.

The fog came down like a cold, wet blanket.

"Hello!" shouted Jonas. "Mrs Berg!"

No one answered. The fog ate their voices.

Then, from far away, they heard a sound. Ding. Ding.

Bells.

"Is there a church up here?" asked Paloma.

"No," said Jonas. "There is no church and no village. There is nothing on this mountain."

## [c2] Shelter

### [p05]
"Don't move," said Jonas. "Rule three. If the fog comes, stop and stay together."

"But we aren't with the class," said Paloma. "We have to find them!"

She walked quickly into the fog. Jonas ran after her. Then he fell between two rocks.

"Oh! My leg!"

Paloma ran back. Jonas sat on the wet ground and held his leg. His face was white.

"Can you stand?"

He tried, but he sat down again. "I can't walk on it. I'm sorry."

"No, I'm sorry," said Paloma. "This is because of me. Me and my flower."

### [p06]
Paloma took out her phone. It did not work up here.

"Mrs Berg is going to look for us," said Jonas. "So we should stay in one place."

The wind was cold, and the fog was wet. Their clothes were wet, too.

"We must keep warm," said Jonas. "My dad says that's the most important thing."

Near the flower, there was a big rock. Slowly, they moved behind it, away from the wind.

Paloma had her orange jacket, a hat and some chocolate. Jonas had a thin silver blanket in his bag.

"Mrs Berg gave everybody one this morning," he said. "Didn't you listen?"

"I was taking photos," said Paloma.

### [p07]
They put the silver blanket around them both. It was warmer. They ate half of the chocolate and kept the other half.

"Tell me something," said Paloma. "Something boring. Then I can forget the fog."

So Jonas told her about his little sister and her three fish. The fish were called One, Two and Three. They were very boring fish.

Paloma laughed. Time passed slowly.

At five o'clock, the fog was darker.

"It's going to be dark at seven," said Jonas. "We can't stay here all night."

Then they heard the bells again. They were closer now.

### [p08]
"What is it?" asked Paloma quietly.

"I don't know." Jonas looked tired. His leg was bigger now.

Paloma tried to remember Mrs Berg's rules. Rule one: stay on the path. Rule two: stay with a friend. Rule three: stop in the fog.

And rule four? She remembered a blue butterfly and a click. Nothing more.

"Jonas, what was rule four?"

Jonas closed his eyes. "Something about listening? I can't think. My leg hurts too much."

Then something moved in the fog. It was a big, dark shape. Then there was another one. And another one.

The shapes were coming towards them.

## [c3] The Bells

### [p09]
Paloma held Jonas's arm. Her heart jumped.

The first shape came closer. It had four thin legs and a long face. It had a little beard. Around its neck, there was a bell.

Then it opened its mouth and made a funny noise.

Paloma started to laugh. "It's a goat! Jonas, it's only a goat!"

There were more behind it. Ten, fifteen, twenty. Brown ones, white ones, black ones. Every animal had a bell.

They walked past the rock, one after another. They were not interested in two cold children.

### [p10]
One small white goat stopped. It looked at Paloma's chocolate paper. Then it ate the paper.

"Hey!" said Paloma. It ran back to the others.

"Look," said Jonas. "They aren't eating grass. They're all walking the same way."

It was true. They walked quickly down the hill, in a long line. They looked like children after school.

"Where do goats go in the evening?" asked Paloma.

"Home," said Jonas. Then his eyes opened wide. "Paloma, goats live on farms. Farms have houses. And houses have people."

### [p11]
"But we don't know these goats," said Paloma. "Maybe they're going to the other side of the mountain."

"Maybe," said Jonas. "But it's half past five. It's going to be dark soon. We can't sit here."

Paloma looked at the fog. Then she looked at the animals.

The last one was big and old. It walked slowly, and its bell had a deep sound.

Paloma thought about Mrs Berg's voice. If you are lost, listen.

She listened. The bells were going down, down, down the mountain.

"OK," Paloma said. She stood up and held out her hand. "We're going to follow them."

## [c4] Home Before Dark

### [p12]
Jonas held Paloma's shoulder and walked on one leg. They followed the old animal with the deep bell.

It was hard. The ground was wet and full of stones.

Paloma gave Jonas the last piece of chocolate.

"You should eat it," he said.

"No. You're the one with the bad leg."

The old goat stopped, too. It looked back and waited for them. Then it walked on.

"I think it likes us," said Jonas.

"I think it wants my hat," said Paloma.

### [p13]
Then the ground was different. It was flat and hard and brown.

"Jonas," said Paloma. "It's the path!"

The fog was thinner now. Paloma saw trees. Then a yellow light between the trees. Then a roof and a gate.

It was the old wooden house. Their house!

The animals went into the field behind the house, one after another. An old man stood at the gate and counted them.

"Eighteen, nineteen, twenty," he said. Then he looked at Paloma and Jonas. "And two more?"

### [p14]
Mrs Berg ran out of the house. Half the class ran behind her.

"Paloma! Jonas!" She held them both. "We looked for you for three hours! Are you hurt?"

"Jonas hurt his leg," said Paloma. "I'm really sorry. I left the path for a photo."

Mrs Berg looked at his leg. "It isn't broken. You're going to be fine. But how did you find the house in this fog?"

"We followed the goats," said Jonas.

Mrs Berg smiled for the first time. "Of course you did. Rule four."

"Rule four?" asked Paloma.

"If you are lost, listen. Follow the bells. These goats come home at six every evening. I told you this morning."

Jonas laughed. "I heard it. Then I hurt my leg, and I forgot."

Paloma's face went red. "I didn't hear it. I was taking a photo of a butterfly."

### [p15]
That evening, Jonas sat by the fire. His leg was on a chair. The old man brought them hot milk. It was goat's milk, of course.

Paloma looked at her photos. The white flower was beautiful, but it was not her favourite now.

In her favourite, twenty goats walked in a line through the fog. The old one was at the back. It looked straight at the camera.

"That's the best photo of the trip," said Jonas.

"I know," said Paloma. "But next time, I'm going to listen first. Then I'm going to take the photo."

Outside, in the dark field, a bell rang softly. Ding.
