# Good Morning, Wrong Number
> A2-K05 · Günlük hayat/duygu · H-071 metni (taslak, kullanıcı onayı bekliyor).
> Kurallar: cümle ≤ 12 kelime; past simple, because / so, sıklık zarfları, kısa mesaj dili ve tam cümle. İşaretler: docs/KITAPLIK.md §6.2.

## [c1] Seven O'Clock

### [p01]
Every morning at seven, a stranger sent Mateo the sea.

It started on the first Monday of the summer. Mateo was in bed. His phone made a small sound.

There was a photo from a number he didn't know. The water was green and blue, and the sky was pink.

Under the photo, there was a message. "Good morning, little sister! Look, the sea is quiet today."

Mateo read it twice. He was fifteen, and he was a boy. He had no sister.

So who was this person?

### [p02]
Mateo lived on a small island in the Atlantic. His house was yellow, with a blue balcony. From the balcony, he could see the beach.

Usually, Mateo spent every summer in the water. But this summer was different. Three days before, he fell off his bike. Now his broken leg was in a heavy white cast.

"Six weeks," the doctor said. "No swimming, no football, no beach."

Six weeks! That was the whole summer!

His friends were away, too. Rui was with his grandparents, and Leo was at a camp. Mateo was alone in his room with his leg.

### [p03]
On the first day, Mateo watched TV. On the third day, he looked at the wall.

"I'm so boring," he told his mum.

Mum laughed. "No, you're bored. Boring is different."

She sat on his bed. "Show me that photo again. The one from the stranger."

Mateo showed her. He always told his mum about strange messages. It was a family rule.

"It's our beach," said Mum. "Look, there are the black rocks."

"Should I answer?" asked Mateo.

"Not yet," said Mum. "Never send photos of yourself. Never give our address. And always show me first."

### [p04]
The photos came every morning at seven. On Tuesday, there was a white bird on the water. On Wednesday, there were small fishing boats.

Every message started with the same words. "Good morning, little sister!"

Mateo often thought about his first swimming lesson. He was six. A tall woman with a red hat taught him. She had a whistle and a kind laugh.

"Head up, Mateo!" she shouted. "Kick your legs!"

He was afraid of the waves, but she was never angry.

On Friday, Mateo woke up at seven. He looked at his phone.

Nothing.

At eight, there was still no photo. At nine, there was nothing again. Why did the photos stop?

## [c2] Wrong Number

### [p05]
On Saturday morning, there was still no photo.

Mateo went slowly to the kitchen. "Mum, the photos stopped," he said.

"You miss them," said Mum with a small smile.

"Maybe," said Mateo. "Can I write to the number now? Just one message?"

"OK. But we write it together. And no names."

Mateo quickly typed, "wrong nr pls dont stop."

Mum shook her head. "This is a stranger, not Rui. Write real sentences, please."

So Mateo wrote it again, slowly and carefully.

"Hello. I'm sorry, but this is the wrong number. Please don't stop, because I love your photos."

He sent it. Then he waited.

### [p06]
An hour later, the phone made a sound.

"Oh no! I'm so sorry!" the message said. "My sister moved to another country in May. She got a new number. So I sent my photos to you!"

Then another message came. "Do you like the sea?"

Mateo looked at Mum. "I love it," he wrote. "But I broke my leg. I can't go out this summer."

"Poor you!" came the answer. "Then I'm going to send you the sea every morning. Is that OK?"

Mateo smiled for the first time in a week. "Yes, please," he wrote.

### [p07]
So the photos started again. Now there were words with them, too.

"Look, dolphins! Three of them came very close today."

"This red boat goes out at six. The old fisherman always waves."

"The water is colder today, so only two people swam."

Mateo read every message to Mum at breakfast.

"How does this person know about the swimmers?" asked Mum.

"Maybe they live near the beach," said Mateo.

The stranger never asked for Mateo's name or address. Mum liked that.

Mateo asked questions, too. "Why do you get up so early?"

"Because I work at the beach," came the answer. "My job is to watch the sea."

### [p08]
One morning, the photo was different. On the left, there were houses on the hill.

"Good morning!" said the message. "Today I can see a yellow house with a blue balcony. It's the brightest house on the hill."

Mateo looked at the photo. Then he looked out of his window.

The yellow house was his house. The blue balcony was his balcony.

He went to the kitchen as quickly as he could.

"Mum! The stranger can see our house!"

## [c3] The Photos

### [p09]
Mum looked at the photo carefully. Then she went out to the balcony.

"Come here," she said. "Look at the beach. What can you see?"

Mateo looked. He saw the sand, the black rocks, the café and many people.

"Everyone on that beach can see our house," said Mum. "It's yellow, so it's very easy to see. This person doesn't know it's ours."

Mateo felt better. Mum was right.

"But now I want to know something," he said. "Where does this person take the photos?"

Mum smiled. "Then find the answer. You have a lot of time."

### [p10]
Mateo looked at all the photos again, one by one.

They all showed the same beach, with the same black rocks. They were all from high up, too. The swimmers looked very small.

"The person is somewhere high," he told Mum. "Higher than the café, but lower than our hill."

"A tree?" asked Mum.

"There aren't any trees on the beach."

### [p11]
Mateo took his own photo from the balcony. Then he compared it with the stranger's photos. His photo was from the side. The stranger's photos were from the middle of the beach.

He found other small things, too. In one photo, there was a red float. In another photo, there was a wooden step in the corner.

Then a new message came.

"A little boy was in the water today. He was afraid of the waves. I told him, 'Head up! Kick your legs!' He did very well."

Mateo read it again. Head up. Kick your legs.

He knew those words. But from where?

### [p12]
It was the last week of the summer holiday. The doctor was going to take off the cast on Friday.

On Thursday, the photo came at seven, as usual. But this time, it wasn't the sea.

It was an empty red chair, high above the sand. On the chair, there was an old red hat.

Under the photo, there was a short message. "Tomorrow is my last day here. Thank you for watching the sea with me."

The cast was going to come off tomorrow. And tomorrow, he was going to find the stranger.

## [c4] The Tower

### [p13]
On Friday morning, the doctor took off the cast. Mateo's leg was thin and white. It felt strange and weak.

"I'm coming with you to the beach," said Mum. "That's the rule."

"I know," said Mateo. But Mum had a strange smile on her face. Did she know something?

They walked down the hill together.

The beach was full of people. In the middle of the beach, there was a tall white tower. At the top, there was a red chair.

### [p14]
A woman stood at the bottom of the tower. She was tall, with grey hair under an old red hat. She had a whistle around her neck.

She looked at Mateo's leg. Then she looked at his face.

"Head up, Mateo," she said with a big smile.

Mateo stopped. He knew that voice. He knew that laugh.

"Ines?" he said. "You taught me to swim!"

"Yes, I did," said Ines. "You were a very brave little boy. You were afraid, but you never stopped."

"The photos were from your tower!" said Mateo. "It was you!"

"And it was you!" laughed Ines. "I didn't know until today."

Mum laughed, too. "I guessed last week. I heard 'Head up! Kick your legs!' all summer, nine years ago."

### [p15]
They sat on the sand next to the tower. Ines told them her story.

For thirty years, she watched the sea from this tower. Every morning, she took a photo for her little sister. But in May, her sister moved to a cold country far away.

"She missed the sea," said Ines. "So I sent it to her. Then I sent it to the wrong number. But my best swimmer saw it."

"Is today really your last day?" asked Mateo.

"Yes. A new lifeguard starts tomorrow." She looked at him. "But next summer, we're going to train young volunteers. They help the lifeguards. Do you want to try?"

Mateo looked at the sea. It was green and blue, and very quiet.

"Yes," he said. "Yes, please."

The next morning at seven, his phone made a small sound. There was a photo of the sea and a message.

"Good morning, little brother! Head up. See you next summer."
